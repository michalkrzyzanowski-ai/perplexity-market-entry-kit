# Skill 06 — Create executive deck

## Purpose

Translate the reviewed decision draft into a concise, visual executive presentation without changing the evidence base.

## Required inputs

- `decision/revised-decision-brief.md` or `decision/market-entry-draft.md`
- `decision/evidence-ledger.md`
- `decision/review-findings.md`
- `decision/unresolved-items.md`
- `templates/executive-deck-outline.md`

## Design rules

- Use conclusion-led slide headlines.
- Use one primary message and one primary visual per slide.
- Keep the main deck to 6–8 slides.
- Use details, citations, definitions, and limitations in speaker notes or an appendix.
- Do not introduce new factual claims, numbers, or recommendations.
- Label estimates, assumptions, unknowns, and human-review status visibly.
- Keep market attractiveness separate from company fit.

## Required slides

1. Decision and options
2. Why company context changes the analysis
3. Decision criteria and key constraints
4. Market attractiveness versus company fit
5. Evidence confidence and counter-evidence
6. Options comparison: option A, option B, postponement/no-go
7. Conditional recommendation or insufficient-evidence finding
8. Next validation actions, owners, and human decision gate

## Suggested visuals

- Before/after company-context comparison
- Attractiveness-versus-fit matrix
- Readiness-dependency map
- Option-comparison table
- Evidence-confidence legend: supported, conditional, missing, goal/assumption

## Output

Create `decision/executive-deck-outline.md` with for each slide:

- Slide number
- Conclusion-led title
- Business message
- Proposed visual
- Supporting evidence IDs/company files
- Speaker notes
- Limitations or caveats

## Do not

- Do not create false quantitative charts from qualitative evidence.
- Do not hide unresolved risks in an appendix.
- Do not label the presentation approved.

## Human gate

A human reviews the deck before it is used for an executive decision or shared externally.
